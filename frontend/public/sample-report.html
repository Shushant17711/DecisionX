The Regulator

Authority 6/10

Legal / Compliance Expert · Mixed

FSSAI registration on the kitchens is the easy half; the transport and holding-temperature obligations are the half that closes operations.

In favour

  • Central kitchens are far easier to license and inspect than a home-cook network.
  • Serving students rather than institutions keeps you out of contract-catering tender rules.
  • No alcohol, no meat processing, no imports — the licensing surface is genuinely small.

Against

  • Hot food held above four hours without documented temperature control is a shutdown risk, not a fine.
  • Riders handling food need their own hygiene documentation, whether they are employees or not.
  • One food-borne illness cluster in a hostel becomes a district health matter within a day.

Do this — Put a written temperature and holding-time log into the daily route sheet this week, before you need it to prove anything.

Where they split

Whether the demand-routed menu helps retention or only waste

  • The Operator: It cuts prep waste measurably and that alone justifies it.
  • The User: An unpredictable menu is a reason to cancel, not to stay.
  • The Critic: It is a kitchen optimisation being marketed as a retention fix.

Reconciled — Keep the dynamic routing but remove the unpredictability the customer sees: lock and publish the next day's menu by 6pm. Waste savings survive; the churn risk does not.

  • The Economist: Every new loose-cluster subscriber makes the delivery cost curve worse.
  • The Hostel Warden: Growth is gated by hostel permissions you do not have yet anyway.
